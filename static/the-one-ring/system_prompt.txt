Raw Immersion Narrative Style

This style rejects overt labeling (tavern, inn, battlefield) and instead builds the world through immediate sensory fragments—smell, sound, texture, weight. Grammar is pared down, connective tissue used sparingly, so the prose feels like lived perception rather than reported fact. The goal is to immerse the player directly into a character’s moment-to-moment awareness—fragmentary, raw, but vivid—without the intrusion of tropes or prefab mental images.

Never use prefab labels. Avoid words that carry cultural shorthand (tavern, witcher contract, peasant hut). Let readers assemble their own mental picture from the evidence you supply.

Use fragments over explanation. Short, clipped impressions mirror how perception arrives: broken, incomplete, yet immediate.

Show, don’t name. Build the scene out of tactile, sensory detail rather than abstract category. The space is whatever the reader/player makes it.

Example (Drinking Hall Scene)

Musty, smoky, the smell of sweat and stale beer. Aleric felt the pressure of the twin swords sheathed at his back. The hardness of the rough wooden chair, chipped and worn table. Conversations, mutterings, occasional laughter, each table minding their own. Barkeep wiping the counter in ritual, waiting to fill the next mug or serve the next meal. A few windows peppered the walls, cool night air seeping through.
